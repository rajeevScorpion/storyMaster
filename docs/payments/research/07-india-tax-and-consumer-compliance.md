## 1. GST classification and rate (SAC, subscription vs coin pack)

## 2. Prepaid coins — voucher, advance, or supply at purchase? Breakage

**Primary-adjacent finding, high relevance to Kissago's core coin model:**

CBIC **Circular No. 243/37/2024-GST, dated 31 December 2024** (issued pursuant to the 55th GST Council meeting) clarifies GST treatment of vouchers:
- Transactions in a **voucher itself are not a supply of goods or services**. If the voucher is an RBI-recognized Pre-Paid Instrument (PPI), it is treated as "money" (excluded from goods/services). If not RBI-recognized, it is treated as an **actionable claim** under **Schedule III, Entry 6, CGST Act** — also excluded from taxable supply.
- **Breakage (unredeemed/expired voucher value) is NOT taxable under GST** — CBIC states unredeemed value generates no GST liability, and non-redemption cannot be deemed a supply of services absent an explicit contractual charge for non-redemption.
- Distribution economics: principal-to-principal resale of vouchers → no GST on the trading margin; agent/commission-based distribution → GST applies only to the commission/fee (this is a service).
- Consistent with this, the **Finance Act, 2025** reportedly **omitted the voucher-specific time-of-supply sub-sections** (old Sections 12(4)/13(4) CGST Act) since vouchers are no longer treated as a "supply" event at all.
- Primary PDF: https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-01/circular-no-243-2024.pdf (fetch attempted this session — returned as an undecoded binary PDF stream, could not extract text directly; relied on SECONDARY (reliable) synthesis instead: [Centax Online](https://www.centaxonline.com/blog/gst-on-vouchers-cbic-clarifies-taxability-supply-status-breakage-treatment), [CashFlo](https://www.cashflo.io/magazine/gst-treatment-of-vouchers-circular-dated-31-12-2024), [Vatsalya B & Co](https://www.vatsalyabco.com/gst-vouchers-cbic-circular-243.html)). **Re-fetch the primary PDF with a different extraction method before finalizing — do not quote circular language to the owner/CA without primary confirmation.**

**ASK CA — this is the single most consequential open question for Kissago's product model:**
The circular's "voucher" reasoning contemplates instruments redeemable for goods/services potentially across multiple SKUs or even multiple merchants (gift cards, PPIs). Kissago's "coins" are a **closed-loop, single-issuer, non-transferable virtual currency**, purchased specifically to consume Kissago's own AI actions (story text, images, narration, video export) — arguably closer to an **advance payment for a continuous supply of service by the same supplier** than to a "voucher" in the sense the circular addresses. That distinction matters enormously:
- If coins = voucher (not a supply): GST is due only when coins are *spent* on a taxable action, and unspent/expired coin balance is untaxed breakage.
- If coins = advance payment for services: **Section 13(2), CGST Act** (time of supply of services) still taxes an advance **at time of receipt** if an invoice isn't issued within the prescribed period — GST could be argued due **at the moment of coin purchase**, not at redemption. (Confirmed only via secondary/CBIC-repository text this session: [CBIC tax repository, Section 13](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter4/section13_v1.00.html) — the widely-cited 2018/2019 amendment removed a cross-reference, not the substantive advance-payment rule.)
- Practically most Indian SaaS/gaming platforms selling "coins/credits" (e.g., mobile games, in-app currencies) treat the coin sale itself as taxable at purchase (invoice + GST charged on the top-up), which is the conservative, lower-risk position — but this creates friction with the voucher circular's "no supply until redemption" logic if a regulator later reclassifies coins as vouchers (refund/reversal complexity). **This is squarely an ASK CA item**: get an explicit opinion on how Kissago's coin model should be classified and invoiced (at purchase vs at redemption), and whether an advance ruling (AAR) is worth seeking given transaction volumes.
- Not yet researched: any RBI angle on whether closed-loop, non-cash-out in-app coins even qualify as a "Prepaid Payment Instrument" needing separate RBI treatment (distinct from the GST question) — likely out of scope (closed-system PPIs below certain thresholds are typically exempt from RBI PPI licensing), but flag for the CA/compliance review anyway.

## 3. Registration threshold and pre-registration state

- General GST registration threshold for **services**: **Section 22, CGST Act** — **₹20 lakh** aggregate turnover (all-India basis), **₹10 lakh** for specified special-category states.
- **Section 24, CGST Act** normally forces mandatory registration for *any* inter-state taxable supply regardless of turnover — but **Notification No. 10/2017-Integrated Tax, dated 13 October 2017** specifically **exempts persons making inter-state supply of services (not goods)** from this mandatory-registration trigger, so long as aggregate all-India turnover (including export receipts) stays under the ₹20 lakh/₹10 lakh threshold. SECONDARY (reliable) summaries: [Udyamica](https://udyamica.com/2021/02/Inter-state-supply-of-services-up-to-rs-20-lakh-allowed-without-GST-registration.html), [Hubco](https://www.hubco.in/articles/notification-no-10-2017-exemption-to-certain-category-of-persons-from-obtaining-registration-under-the-act); primary text: https://keralataxes.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/10_2017_IT.pdf (not yet fetched directly this session).
- **Practical implication for Kissago**: being an all-India online service does **not** by itself force GST registration before ₹20 lakh turnover — the interstate-goods trap (Section 24) does not apply to a pure service business under this notification. Below threshold, Kissago **cannot charge GST on invoices** (only a registered person can) — pricing/invoices pre-registration should show no GST line, not a "GST not applicable — unregistered" disclaimer is a good practice (SHOULD, not yet source-confirmed as a specific CBIC requirement — check).
- **Section 14, IGST Act** (the "simplified OIDAR registration" scheme) is for **non-resident** OIDAR suppliers (e.g., Netflix, foreign SaaS) supplying to Indian consumers, registering in India via a simplified process — this is **not** the route Kissago (an Indian-domiciled supplier) would use; Kissago registers as an ordinary domestic taxpayer once required. Worth a one-line confirmation from the CA since Kissago's supply is still functionally "OIDAR-classified" (SAC-wise) even though it doesn't use the foreign-OIDAR registration mechanism.
- Not yet checked: whether crossing ₹20 lakh triggers *compulsory* same-month registration or gives a grace period; exact process/timeline (typically registration required within 30 days of becoming liable).

## 4. Tax invoice rules

- **Rule 46, CGST Rules 2017** prescribes ~16 mandatory fields on a tax invoice: consecutive serial number **max 16 characters, unique per financial year**; date; supplier name/address/GSTIN; recipient name/address/GSTIN (if registered); **for unregistered ("B2C") recipients where invoice value ≥ ₹50,000, recipient's name, address and place-of-supply state+code become mandatory too**; HSN/SAC code; description; quantity; taxable value; rate and amount of tax (CGST+SGST or IGST, itemized); place of supply (for interstate); whether tax is payable on reverse charge; signature/digital signature of supplier or authorized representative. SECONDARY (reliable), not yet cross-checked against the bare Rule text: [GimBooks](https://www.gimbooks.com/blog/gst-invoice-mandatory-fields-rule-46-checklist/), [ZapInvoice](https://zapinvoice.in/blog/gst-invoice-format-requirements). **Primary Rule 46 text not yet fetched — do this before finalizing the field checklist for the owner.**
- **Directly on point for Kissago — CBIC Circular No. 242/36/2024-GST, dated 31 December 2024** (companion to the voucher circular, same GST Council meeting): clarifies that under the **proviso to Rule 46(f)**, for **online services supplied to unregistered recipients** — CBIC's own example list explicitly includes **e-books, cloud services, OTT/entertainment subscriptions, e-newspapers/e-magazines, online telecom services, and digital services through mobile applications, and online gaming** — the supplier **must record the recipient's State name on the invoice, regardless of invoice value** (i.e., the ₹50,000 B2C threshold does NOT apply to this specific field for online services — state capture is mandatory on every transaction). This recorded state is then **deemed the "address on record"** for place-of-supply purposes (see §5). **Failure to comply risks a penalty under Section 122(3)(e), CGST Act.** SECONDARY (reliable), consistent across multiple sources: [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/clarification-place-supply-online-services-unregistered-recipients.html), [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/clarification-on-place-supply-online-services-supplied-by-suppliers-services-to-unregistered-recipients), [Centax Online](https://www.centaxonline.com/blog/gst-compliance-for-online-services-cbic-clarification-on-place-of-supply-for-unregistered-recipients). Primary PDF (not yet text-extracted): https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-01/circular-no-242-2024.pdf.
  - **This is a MUST-level, concrete product requirement**: Kissago's checkout must capture the customer's state (not infer it from card BIN/IP) and store it against every invoice, for every coin-pack and subscription purchase, once GST-registered.
- **E-invoicing (IRN) threshold**: multiple 2025-dated secondary sources claim the aggregate-turnover threshold is **₹5 crore currently, with a reported reduction to ₹2 crore effective 1 October 2025**, plus a separate **30-day IRP reporting deadline for taxpayers ≥ ₹10 crore turnover effective 1 April 2025**. **None of this was confirmed against a primary CBIC/GSTN notification this session — treat as UNCONFIRMED.** Regardless of exact threshold, Kissago is very unlikely to cross even the lower reported figure at launch, so e-invoicing is not a go-live blocker, but the exact figure matters for the multi-year roadmap and should be verified from GSTN/CBIC directly before the owner is told a number.

SECONDARY (reliable): [GSTZen](https://gstzen.in/b2c-invoice/b2c-qr-code.html), [IRIS GST](https://irisgst.com/e-invoicing-and-b2c-transactions-qr-code), [Haacas](https://haacas.com/dynamic-code-for-b2c-invoices-if-turnover-more-than-rs-500-crore/).

## 5. Place of supply for B2C online services

- **Section 12(2), IGST Act** (general rule, supplier and recipient both in India, recipient unregistered): place of supply = **location of recipient where address on record exists**; else, **location of the supplier**. "Address on record" = the address of the recipient as available in the supplier's records. SECONDARY (reliable) restating Act text: [The Learn Notes](https://thelearnnotes.com/blog/place-of-supply-of-services-general-rules-section-12-2-), [TaxTMI compiler](https://www.taxtmi.com/manuals?id=1928).
- **Circular 242/36/2024-GST (31 Dec 2024)** — see §4 — overrides the informal practice of inferring place of supply from card/bank/IP data: for online services (OIDAR-type) to unregistered recipients, the customer's **State, as recorded by the supplier on the invoice** (mandatory field per the Rule 46(f) proviso), **is** the address on record / place of supply, by deeming fiction. This is a clean, low-ambiguity rule **if Kissago actually collects state at checkout** — CGST+SGST applies if that state equals Kissago's registered state; IGST otherwise.
- **Practical answer to "what minimal data must Kissago collect at checkout"**: a **self-declared state of residence** field, captured and stored per transaction (a dropdown, ideally sourced from `FilterDropdown` per repo convention — implementation detail for the codebase stream, not this one). Billing/card address or IP geolocation are **not** the compliant basis per the circular — the state must be *recorded* by the supplier as part of the invoice.
- Not yet researched: what happens if the declared state conflicts with other known signals (e.g., GST fraud/evasion risk) — likely an ASK CA nuance, not researched this session.
