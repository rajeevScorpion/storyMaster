## 1. GST classification and rate (SAC, subscription vs coin pack)

- Digital/AI content-generation services of this kind (automated delivery, minimal human intervention) fall under **OIDAR** — "Online Information and Database Access or Retrieval" services, defined in **Section 2(17), IGST Act 2017**. SECONDARY (reliable): [ClearTax — OIDAR GST Registration](https://cleartax.in/s/oidar-gst-registration), [Dewan P N Chopra & Co](https://www.dpncindia.com/gst-provisions-on-online-information-and-database-access-or-retrieval-oidar-services), [CaptainBiz](https://www.captainbiz.com/blogs/oidar-services-under-gst-understanding-tax-implication-and-compliance-guidelines/).
- Commonly cited **SAC code 998439** ("other online content") under heading 9984, at the general **18% GST rate**; e-books are carved out at 5%. This SAC/rate pairing recurs across secondary sources, but the exact SAC digits and current rate were **not independently confirmed against the primary rate notification (11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), as amended)** — treat SAC digits as UNCONFIRMED.
- **GST 2.0 rate rationalization**, effective **22 September 2025**: 0/5/12/18/28% collapsed to **0/5/18/40%** — overwhelmingly a **goods** reshuffle (12%→5%, most of 28%→18%, luxury/sin→40%). No evidence found that standard 18% services (SAC 9983/9984) moved. SECONDARY (reliable): [EY India](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2025/09/gst-council-announces-major-rate-rationalization-and-trade-facilitation-measures). **Still not primary-confirmed that OIDAR/SaaS specifically stayed at 18% — low risk, but verify before quoting a rate in a pricing page.**
- **Subscription (coin grant) vs one-time top-up coin pack**: structurally the same SAC/rate (18%) once/if coins are treated as consideration for an OIDAR-classified service. The real open question is not rate but *timing* — see §2.

## 2. Prepaid coins — voucher, advance, or supply at purchase? Breakage

**High relevance to Kissago's core coin model.**

CBIC **Circular No. 243/37/2024-GST, dated 31 December 2024** (55th GST Council meeting) clarifies GST treatment of vouchers:
- A **voucher transaction is not itself a supply of goods or services**. An RBI-recognized Pre-Paid Instrument (PPI) voucher is "money" (excluded from goods/services); a non-PPI voucher is an **actionable claim** under **Schedule III, Entry 6, CGST Act** — also excluded from taxable supply.
- **Breakage (unredeemed/expired voucher value) is NOT taxable under GST.** Non-redemption is not deemed a supply of services absent an explicit contractual charge for non-redemption.
- Principal-to-principal resale of vouchers → no GST on the margin; agent/commission distribution → GST only on the commission (a service).
- The **Finance Act, 2025** reportedly omitted the voucher-specific time-of-supply sub-sections (old Sections 12(4)/13(4)), consistent with "not a supply."
- Primary PDF (text extraction failed twice this session — undecoded binary stream both attempts): https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-01/circular-no-243-2024.pdf. SECONDARY (reliable): [Centax Online](https://www.centaxonline.com/blog/gst-on-vouchers-cbic-clarifies-taxability-supply-status-breakage-treatment), [CashFlo](https://www.cashflo.io/magazine/gst-treatment-of-vouchers-circular-dated-31-12-2024), [Vatsalya B & Co](https://www.vatsalyabco.com/gst-vouchers-cbic-circular-243.html).

**ASK CA — the single most consequential open question for Kissago's product model:**
The circular's "voucher" reasoning contemplates instruments redeemable for goods/services potentially across multiple SKUs or merchants (gift cards, PPIs). Kissago's coins are a **closed-loop, single-issuer, non-transferable virtual currency**, sold specifically to consume Kissago's own AI actions — arguably closer to an **advance payment for continuous supply of service by the same supplier** than to the circular's "voucher." That distinction changes everything:
- Coins = voucher → GST due only when coins are *spent*; unspent/expired balance is untaxed breakage.
- Coins = advance payment for services → **Section 13(2), CGST Act** taxes an advance **at time of receipt**, i.e., GST could be due **at the moment of coin purchase**, not at redemption (SECONDARY-confirmed via [CBIC tax repository, Section 13](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter4/section13_v1.00.html); the 2018/2019 amendment removed a cross-reference, not the substantive advance-payment rule).
- **Concrete product fact that sharpens this**: Kissago's subscription coins *expire at cycle end* while top-up coins *never expire*, and *refunds do not claw back coins already granted*. This is exactly the kind of fact pattern a CA/AAR needs — expiring subscription coins look more like a rolling service entitlement (arguably supports "advance for service," taxed at grant), while never-expiring top-up coins look more like the circular's "voucher" (arguably supports "not a supply until redeemed"). **The two coin types may legitimately need different GST treatment from each other** — this should be put to the CA explicitly, not assumed to be one uniform answer.
- Most Indian SaaS/gaming platforms selling "coins/credits" tax the top-up at purchase (conservative, lower audit risk) — but this creates friction with the voucher circular's logic if a regulator later reclassifies coins as vouchers (refund/reversal complexity). Get this in writing before finalizing pricing/invoicing logic; consider whether transaction volume justifies an Advance Ruling (AAR).

## 3. Registration threshold and pre-registration state

- **Section 22, CGST Act**: services registration threshold **₹20 lakh** aggregate turnover (all-India), **₹10 lakh** for specified special-category states.
- **Section 24, CGST Act** would normally force registration for *any* inter-state taxable supply regardless of turnover, but **Notification No. 10/2017-Integrated Tax, dated 13 October 2017** exempts persons making **inter-state supply of services** (not goods) from that trigger, so long as all-India turnover (including exports) stays under the ₹20 lakh/₹10 lakh threshold. SECONDARY (reliable): [Udyamica](https://udyamica.com/2021/02/Inter-state-supply-of-services-up-to-rs-20-lakh-allowed-without-GST-registration.html), [Hubco](https://www.hubco.in/articles/notification-no-10-2017-exemption-to-certain-category-of-persons-from-obtaining-registration-under-the-act); primary text (not fetched this session): https://keralataxes.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/10_2017_IT.pdf.
- **Practical implication**: being an all-India online service does **not** by itself force GST registration before ₹20 lakh turnover. Below threshold, Kissago **cannot charge GST on invoices** (only a registered person may).
- **Section 14, IGST Act** (simplified OIDAR registration) is for **non-resident** OIDAR suppliers (Netflix-style) — not the route an Indian-domiciled Kissago would use once liable; it registers as an ordinary domestic taxpayer, even though its supply remains OIDAR-classified for SAC/rate purposes. Worth a one-line CA confirmation.

## 4. Tax invoice rules

- **Rule 46, CGST Rules 2017** — could not be fetched as primary text this session (certificate error on the CBIC repository, and gstcouncil PDF failures elsewhere); relying on consistently-repeated secondary summaries: consecutive serial number, **max 16 characters, unique per financial year**; date; supplier name/address/GSTIN; recipient details (GSTIN if registered; for unregistered/"B2C" where invoice value **≥ ₹50,000**, name/address/place-of-supply state+code); HSN/SAC; description; quantity; taxable value; CGST+SGST or IGST itemized; place of supply (interstate); reverse-charge flag; signature/digital signature. **Re-verify the exact field list against bare Rule text before publishing an invoice-field checklist as final.**
- **Directly on point for Kissago — CBIC Circular No. 242/36/2024-GST, dated 31 December 2024** (companion to the voucher circular): under the **proviso to Rule 46(f)**, for **online services to unregistered recipients** — CBIC's own examples explicitly include e-books, cloud services, OTT subscriptions, e-newspapers/magazines, telecom, **digital services through mobile applications, and online gaming** — the supplier **must record the recipient's State on the invoice regardless of value** (the ₹50,000 threshold does not gate this particular field for online services). That recorded state is then **deemed the "address on record"** for place-of-supply (§5). **Non-compliance risks a penalty under Section 122(3)(e), CGST Act.** SECONDARY (reliable, consistent across sources): [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/clarification-place-supply-online-services-unregistered-recipients.html), [VJM Global](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/clarification-on-place-supply-online-services-supplied-by-suppliers-services-to-unregistered-recipients), [Centax Online](https://www.centaxonline.com/blog/gst-compliance-for-online-services-cbic-clarification-on-place-of-supply-for-unregistered-recipients).
- **Time limit to issue an invoice for services**: **Section 31(2) CGST Act read with Rule 47** — within **30 days** of the date of supply of service (**45 days** for banks/NBFCs/insurers). Late issuance risks Section 122 penalty, 18% p.a. interest on delayed tax, and the recipient's ITC eligibility. SECONDARY (reliable, consistent): [GetSwipe](https://getswipe.in/blog/article/time-limit-for-issue-of-invoice-under-gst-rule-47), [TaxBuddy](https://www.taxbuddy.com/blog/section-31-of-the-cgst-act).
- **Continuous supply of services (directly relevant to auto-renewing subscriptions) — Section 31(5), CGST Act**: (a) where the payment due date is ascertainable from the contract, invoice on/before that due date; (b) where not ascertainable, invoice before/at the time payment is received; (c) where payment is linked to an event's completion, invoice on/before that date. If supply ceases before the contract's completion, invoice at the time of cessation, for the value supplied so far. SECONDARY (reliable): [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=704628), primary-adjacent [CBIC tax repository, Section 31](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter7/section31_v1.00.html). **Practical read for Kissago's auto-renewing subscription**: each renewal charge is its own invoice trigger point (payment date), not a single invoice for the whole subscription term.
- **E-invoicing (IRN) threshold**: secondary sources (not primary-confirmed this session) claim ₹5 crore currently, with a reported drop to **₹2 crore effective 1 October 2025**, plus a 30-day IRP-reporting rule for turnover ≥ ₹10 crore from 1 April 2025. **UNCONFIRMED against a primary CBIC/GSTN notification.** Irrelevant to Kissago at anything like launch scale either way.
- **Dynamic QR code on B2C invoices**: Notification 14/2020-CT (as amended) — only above **₹500 crore** turnover, effective 1 December 2020. **Not relevant to Kissago now.**
- **Digital signature on electronic invoices**: not independently researched this session beyond the general Rule 46 reference to "signature or digital signature of the supplier"; the IRN/e-invoicing scheme's own QR+IRN mechanism substitutes for a signature at that scale. Below the e-invoicing threshold, ordinary practice is a scanned/typed signature block or an authorized-signatory statement on a PDF invoice — **not independently confirmed as strictly sufficient; flag for the CA** if Kissago wants certainty here.

## 5. Place of supply for B2C online services

- **Section 12(2), IGST Act** (supplier and recipient both in India, recipient unregistered): place of supply = **location of recipient where address on record exists**; else, **location of the supplier**. "Address on record" = the recipient's address as available in the supplier's records.
- **Circular 242/36/2024-GST (31 Dec 2024)** (see §4) overrides inferring place of supply from card/bank/IP data: for online (OIDAR-type) services to unregistered recipients, the customer's **State, as recorded by the supplier on the invoice**, **is** the address on record / place of supply by deeming fiction. CGST+SGST applies if that state equals Kissago's registered state; IGST otherwise.
- **Concrete answer to "what minimal data must Kissago collect at checkout"**: a **self-declared state of residence**, captured and stored per transaction. Billing/card address or IP geolocation are **not** the compliant basis per the circular — the state must be *recorded by the supplier* as an invoice field.
- **Code-audit tie-in**: the app currently collects **no** name, address, state, GSTIN, or phone for tax purposes. Once GST-registered, this is a **MUST-fix gap**, not a nice-to-have — Kissago cannot lawfully determine CGST+SGST vs. IGST, nor satisfy Rule 46(f)'s proviso, without a state field at checkout. This is the single cheapest, highest-leverage checkout change identified in this stream.

## 6. Refunds and credit notes

- **Section 34, CGST Act**: a credit note may be issued where the taxable value/tax charged on an invoice exceeds what's payable, or the recipient returns the goods/services, or the goods/services are found deficient. **Time limit: by 30 November following the end of the financial year in which the supply was made, or the date of furnishing the relevant annual return, whichever is earlier.** The supplier declares it in the return for the month issued, and reduces output tax liability accordingly, subject to Section 34's conditions (notably: the reduction is disallowed if it would reduce the recipient's already-availed ITC without the recipient reversing it). SECONDARY (reliable): [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/section-34-understanding-credit-notes-gst.html), [GST Gyaan](https://gstgyaan.com/section-34-circumstance-for-issue-of-credit-notes-or-debit-notes-its-time-limit-and-contents), primary-adjacent CBIC Circular 137/07/2020-GST (https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/Circular_Refund_137_7_2020.pdf, not fetched this session).
- **ASK CA — ties directly to the coin-breakage question in §2 and to a concrete Kissago policy fact**: Kissago's refund policy **does not claw back coins already granted** on a subscription/top-up refund. If GST was charged at the point of coin purchase (the conservative approach in §2), refunding cash while letting the customer keep the coins looks less like "return of goods/services" (which is what Section 34 credit notes are built for) and more like a **discretionary goodwill payment/discount** — which may not cleanly support a GST credit note at all. Practically this could mean: (a) Kissago pays a refund but cannot recover the GST it already remitted on that sale via a credit note, or (b) the CA structures it differently (e.g., partial credit note proportionate to unused coins, with the retained coins treated as the still-taxable portion). **This needs an explicit CA answer before the refund policy's tax mechanics are finalized**, not just its consumer-facing wording.
- **Refund & Cancellation policy page is currently a starter draft** (per code audit) — beyond the GST mechanics above, the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules require the refund/return/cancellation policy to actually be published with real terms (§10) — this is both a tax-documentation gap and a consumer-protection gap simultaneously.

## 7. B2B buyers / GSTIN at checkout

- Rule 46 distinguishes B2B invoices (recipient GSTIN + place of supply required, recipient can claim ITC) from B2C (no GSTIN needed, reported as aggregate values in GSTR-1) — see §4.
- **Minimum data Kissago should collect for a B2B sale**: legal/trade name, GSTIN, and billing address of the business, at least as an optional "business purchase" toggle at checkout (most Indian SaaS checkouts default to B2C and let the buyer opt into "I have a GSTIN"). GSTIN has a checksum and can be validated client-side for format, and/or checked against the GST portal's public search for existence — this is common industry practice (SHOULD) but not a strict Rule 46 mandate to validate via API; the mandate is simply that the invoice must correctly state a valid GSTIN if the buyer claims one. Incorrect GSTINs risk the buyer's ITC, not primarily Kissago's liability, but a wrong GSTIN on Kissago's own returns can cause GSTR-1/2B mismatches and buyer complaints. SECONDARY (reliable, industry-practice level, not statute): [Message Central](https://www.messagecentral.com/en-in/blog/gst-verification-api-india), [MyBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/blog/gst-invoice-validation-checklist/).
- **Code-audit tie-in**: since the app currently collects **no** billing identity at all, there is currently **no way** for a business customer to get a compliant B2B invoice (and therefore no way for them to claim ITC) — this is a real lost-revenue/lost-corporate-customer gap, not just a compliance one, once Kissago courts business buyers per the brief's "some may be businesses wanting GST input credit" note.

## 8. Record keeping

- **GST — Section 36, CGST Act**: retain accounts/records for **72 months (6 years) from the due date of furnishing the annual return** for that year (effectively ~7–8 years from the end of the relevant financial year in practice). If under appeal/investigation, retain for one year after final disposal, or the above period, whichever is later. SECONDARY (reliable), consistent across multiple sources including a CBIC-repository mirror: [CBIC tax repository, Section 36](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter8/section36_v1.00.html).
- **Income Tax — Section 44AA-linked practice**: books of account and supporting documents generally retained **6 years from the end of the relevant assessment year** (so ~7 years from the end of the relevant financial year); **8 years** for records relevant to certain specified/transfer-pricing transactions. Penalty for failure to maintain: up to ₹25,000 under Section 271A. SECONDARY (reliable): [Scripbox](https://scripbox.com/tax/section-44aa/), [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/books-of-accounts-and-audit-requirements-for-freelancers).
- **DPDP Act 2023 interaction — the key finding for the "user deletes their account" question**: **Section 8** requires a Data Fiduciary to erase personal data once its purpose is no longer served, **"unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law for the time being in force."** Kissago's GST/Income-tax retention duties (6–8 years, above) are exactly such a legal-retention exception — **DPDP does not require, and does not permit, deleting billing/invoice/order records that GST or Income Tax law still requires be kept**, even on account deletion. SECONDARY (reliable): [DPDPA.com, Section 8 with interpretation](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section8.html), [Digio](https://www.digio.in/blog/right-to-erasure-under-dpdp-what-users-can-demand-and-when/).
  - **This makes the code-audit fact a concrete, dated compliance defect, not a style choice**: `billing_orders`, `billing_subscriptions`, `billing_customers`, and coin-grant records being **cascade-deleted when a user account is deleted** is very likely inconsistent with the GST/Income-tax retention duty above — those specific tables (or an archived/anonymized copy sufficient for tax purposes — legal name, GSTIN if any, state, invoice number, date, amount, tax) need to **survive account deletion for the statutory retention window**, decoupled from the rest of the user's personal data (which DPDP does want erased). **This is a MUST-fix, and probably the single highest-priority engineering change to flag from this whole stream.**
  - Practical shape of the fix (for the codebase stream to size, not this stream's job to design): on account deletion, retain a minimal, tax-law-scoped billing record (or a redacted/archived snapshot) for 6–8 years even as the rest of the account is purged, and disclose this retention in the privacy policy per DPDP's notice requirements.

## 9. Foreign customers (later) — export of services, LUT, foreign digital VAT (brief)

- **Section 2(6), IGST Act** — "export of services" requires, broadly: (1) supplier located in India; (2) recipient located outside India; (3) place of supply outside India; (4) **payment received in convertible foreign exchange, or in INR where RBI permits**; (5) supplier and recipient are not merely establishments of the same distinct person. SECONDARY (reliable): [xFlowPay](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/export-of-services-under-gst), [CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/articles/lut-under-gst-for-export-of-services-complete-guide-to-zerorated-supply-compliance-rule-96a-conditions-rfd11-filing-53653.asp).
- **LUT (Letter of Undertaking)** — filed as Form GST RFD-11 under **Rule 96A**, lets an exporter zero-rate the supply (no IGST charged upfront) while undertaking to pay IGST + interest if export proceeds aren't realized within **1 year of the invoice date**. Miss that window and Kissago would owe the IGST it never charged, plus 18% p.a. interest, with the LUT itself at risk of suspension until cleared.
- **Practical note for Kissago's Razorpay-now/Stripe-later plan**: Razorpay largely settles in INR from Indian card rails, so a foreign customer paying via Razorpay may not straightforwardly satisfy "payment in convertible foreign exchange" — this is exactly the kind of question a CA should confirm before Kissago markets to non-Indian customers on the current payment stack, and is likely a reason a Merchant-of-Record or Stripe-style foreign-currency settlement matters for the export-of-services test itself, not just for UX (stream 8 covers MoR providers in product-capability depth).
- Foreign digital-VAT regimes (EU OSS/IOSS, UK VAT on digital services, similar elsewhere) will eventually apply to Kissago's non-Indian, non-business customers directly — kept intentionally brief here since stream 8 evaluates Merchant-of-Record providers that absorb this obligation.

## 10. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 (+ a 2026 amendment landing soon)

- **Applies regardless of size**: no turnover carve-out found; the Rules apply to "all goods and services bought or sold over digital or electronic network," all e-commerce models (marketplace and inventory), with an exception only for a natural person acting in a purely personal, non-commercial capacity. SECONDARY (reliable): [Legitquest text mirror](https://www.legitquest.com/act/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/91C8"), [Trilegal analysis](https://trilegal.com/knowledge_repository/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/).
- Core obligations (from the 2020 Rules): no price manipulation for unreasonable profit; no discrimination between customers of the same class; disclose total price including all mandatory/voluntary charges, exchange/return/refund terms, delivery/shipment details, expiry/guarantee info where relevant; take back/refund for defective, deficient, late (non-force-majeure), or misdescribed goods/services; **appoint a grievance officer**; no fake/self-authored reviews. SECONDARY (reliable): [Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/knowledge_repository/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules-2020/), [India Law](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/consumer-protection-e-commerce-rules/).
  - **Grievance-officer response timelines specific to this instrument were not confirmed this session** — the commonly-cited 24-hour-acknowledgment/1-month-resolution figures come from the **IT Rules 2021** intermediary framework, a different instrument; do not assume they transfer here without a CA check.
  - **"Shipping/Delivery" for a pure digital service**: Kissago has no physical shipping, but the Rules' disclosure category is "delivery and shipment details" — the practical equivalent for Kissago is disclosing *when and how* access/output is delivered (e.g., "story beats and images generate within N seconds/minutes of purchase; video export renders within N hours"). The code audit found **no Shipping/Delivery policy page exists at all** — even a short "how delivery works for a digital service" statement is a cheap fix and plausibly satisfies the letter of the disclosure category. **ASK CA** whether a digital-only seller can simply state "not applicable — digital delivery" or must still describe delivery mechanics/timing.
- **New and directly relevant: Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026**, notified in September 2026 (per a 14 September 2026 legal-update source, i.e., published days before this research), **taking effect 1 January 2027**:
  - E-commerce entities must **comply with the 2023 Dark Patterns Guidelines (§11), conduct an annual self-audit, and prominently display a compliance certificate.**
  - New "ranking" transparency duties (disclose principal ranking factors in plain language) and price-history duties (any advertised price cut must show the lowest price in the preceding 30 days) — these read as more marketplace/goods-oriented but the dark-patterns self-audit/certificate duty is squarely applicable to any e-commerce entity, direct sellers included.
  - Seller GSTIN/MSME-number disclosure and marketplace-cross-promotion consent duties are marketplace-specific and likely **not applicable** to Kissago as a direct seller of its own service (not a marketplace host for third-party sellers) — but confirm this scoping with a CA once the final gazetted rule text is available, since this summary is drawn from a single secondary legal-update source, not the primary notification.
  - SECONDARY (reliable, very recent, single-source at time of writing): [SCC Online Blog, 14 Sept 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/09/14/consumer-protection-e-commerce-amendment-rules-2026-explained/). **Given it's brand new, worth a direct primary check (Department of Consumer Affairs gazette notification) before treating the 1 Jan 2027 date and exact scope as final** — but the direction of travel (mandatory annual dark-pattern self-audit + public certificate) is clear enough to design for now, comfortably within Kissago's launch runway.

## 11. CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 (+ 2025–2026 developments)

**Primary-adjacent source obtained this session** (a law-firm update that closely tracks/quotes the guideline's own text and Annexure I, page-cited): [Trilegal, "Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023," 26 Dec 2023](https://trilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Guidelines-for-Prevention-and-Regulation-of-Dark-Patterns-2023.pdf).

- **Notified 30 November 2023** by the CCPA under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Applies to: (a) platforms systematically offering goods/services in India (including foreign platforms); (b) advertisers; (c) sellers and service providers. **Scoped to B2C only** — the Consumer Protection Act's own B2C scope means these Guidelines do not reach B2B transactions.
- **All 13 named dark patterns** (Annexure I), with the ones most load-bearing for Kissago's checkout/subscription flow:
  - **Subscription trap**: making cancellation "an impossible or complex process," hiding the cancellation option, **requiring payment details/auto-debit authorization to access a free subscription**, or making cancellation instructions "confusing and cumbersome." Explicitly flagged example: sign-up is self-serve online but cancellation requires calling support — i.e., **cancellation must be at least as easy/available as subscribing.**
  - **SaaS Billing**: "Covertly billing users on a recurrent basis in a SaaS business model **without any notifications**"; the guideline's own example is a **free trial converting to a paid subscription without notifying the user** — this maps almost exactly onto Kissago's current state (no auto-renewal disclosure at checkout, only in Terms; Razorpay's own notification channel switched off via `customer_notify: 0`). **This is as close to a named, on-point violation as this research turned up — treat as MUST-fix, not best practice.**
  - **Drip pricing**: not presenting comprehensive price information upfront — price elements revealed only after confirmation, "free" that hides recurring cost, or requiring an extra purchase to use something already bought. Directly implicated by **"checkout shows no tax disclosure at the point of purchase, only inside Terms."**
  - **Basket sneaking**, **confirm shaming**, **forced action**, **interface interference**, **bait and switch**, **disguised advertisement**, **nagging**, **trick question**, **false urgency**, **rogue malware** — the remaining 10, less directly implicated by what's known of Kissago's flow today but worth a design-review pass.
- **Enforcement**: CCPA can order discontinuance of a practice; non-compliance with a CCPA order carries up to **6 months' imprisonment and/or a fine up to ₹20 lakh**; a misleading advertisement itself carries up to 2 years/₹10 lakh (first offense) rising to 5 years/₹50 lakh (repeat). Consumers can also separately claim compensation through a District Consumer Commission.
- **June 2025**: CCPA advisory directing e-commerce platforms to **self-audit within 3 months** for dark patterns (primary source: [consumeraffairs.gov.in press release PDF](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/admin/cmsfiles/pressRelease/Central_Consumer_Protection_Authority_issues_advisory_to_E-Commerce_Platforms_for_self-audit_within_3_months_to_detect_Dark_Patterns_and_ensure_its_resolutionpress_release.pdf), found via search but not independently fetched this session). This advisory is effectively now **codified** as the annual self-audit + certificate duty in the 2026 Amendment Rules (§10).
- **Active enforcement in 2026**: CCPA has penalized roughly 9 platforms (Zepto, BookMyShow, IndiGo, Physics Wallah, FirstCry, SpiceJet, and others named), ~₹20 lakh recovered as of an August 2026 report — this is not a dormant guideline. SECONDARY: [ANI News, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/govt-says-ccpa-imposes-rs-20-lakh-for-dark-patterns-steps-up-action-against-deceptive-online-practices20260806161831/).

**Concrete do/don't for Kissago's checkout and cancellation, synthesized from the above:**
- **Do** show total price including applicable tax before the payment-method step (drip pricing).
- **Do** disclose auto-renewal terms (amount, frequency, next charge date) at the point of purchase, not only in Terms (SaaS billing / drip pricing).
- **Do** make cancellation reachable in roughly the same number of steps/clicks as subscribing, self-serve, no forced call/email (subscription trap).
- **Don't** require payment-method entry to access something advertised as free, without clearly disclosing it converts to paid (SaaS billing).
- **Don't** rely on Terms-of-Service text alone to satisfy any of the above — the guideline explicitly treats "hidden" or "only in fine print" disclosure as the violation pattern, not merely the absence of disclosure altogether.

## 12. RBI recurring payments / e-mandate rules (summary)

- The RBI **consolidated** its e-mandate rules into the **Digital Payments – E-mandate Framework, 2026** (Circular RBI/DPSS/2026-27/396, dated **21 April 2026**) — this supersedes the earlier piecemeal circulars (2019 origin, 2021 amendments) referenced in older commentary. SECONDARY (reliable, multiple consistent sources): [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/24/rbi-issues-digital-payments-e-mandate-framework-2026/), [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-issues-consolidated-directions-digital-payments-e-mandate-framework-2026.html), [LexOrbis](https://www.lexorbis.com/rbis-digital-payments-e-mandate-framework-2026-consolidated-directions-for-recurring-digital-transactions/); primary circular PDF (not fetched this session): https://www.caalley.com/rbi26/396MDD002E435ECA145509929FC3ACBCFD0E9.pdf (mirror; verify against rbi.org.in directly).
- **AFA (Additional Factor of Authentication) exemption**: pre-authorized recurring transactions up to **₹15,000** can process without a fresh OTP after initial mandate setup; certain categories (SIPs, insurance premiums) reportedly exempted up to ₹1 lakh. **Registering, modifying, or withdrawing** an e-mandate itself always requires AFA.
- **Pre-transaction notification**: the **issuer** (card issuer/UPI PSP) must notify the customer **at least 24 hours before** the debit, and again post-transaction, disclosing merchant name, amount, date/time, the mandate's reference number, and that the debit is pursuant to a registered e-mandate. The customer must be able to choose/change the notification channel (SMS/email) and must have a facility to **opt out of a specific transaction or the mandate entirely**.
- **Who is actually responsible for this reaching the customer — directly relevant to the `customer_notify: 0` fact**: while the **issuer bank** is the primary sender, the framework places compliance obligations on the **merchant and payment aggregator** too — "the acquirer or payment aggregator must ensure that the merchants they onboard comply," and collecting entities are described as directly obligated to provide mandate notifications, opt-out facilities and grievance-redressal details around every debit. SECONDARY: multiple sources including [Chargebee's own compliance documentation of the RBI rule](https://www.chargebee.com/docs/payments/2.0/others/rbi-e-mandate).
  - **Practical read**: even though the bank/issuer is nominally "the notifier," Razorpay's `customer_notify` flag controls a real, additional notification channel that subscription platforms commonly rely on in practice, and the framework's language about aggregator/merchant responsibility for ensuring notifications and opt-out actually reach the customer means **disabling it is a genuine compliance question, not just a UX one** — Kissago should confirm with Razorpay (stream 6) exactly which entity's notification is legally satisfying the 24-hour pre-debit requirement for its specific mandate flow (UPI Autopay vs. card e-mandate can differ), and not assume the bank alone covers it if Razorpay's own channel is off. **Flag as MUST-fix or ASK CA/Razorpay-support pending that confirmation** — this is one of the clearer, checkable action items in this whole brief.

## 13. Minors — contract capacity, DPDP Act/Rules verifiable parental consent

- **Indian Contract Act, 1872, Section 11**: a person must be of the age of majority (18) to be competent to contract. The landmark **Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose (1903)** ruling (still good law) holds an agreement with a minor is **void ab initio** — not merely voidable, and not open to restitution on the minor's side either. SECONDARY (reliable): [SCC Online case explainer](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/06/06/cases-that-made-law-minor-entering-into-binding-contract-mohori-bibee-dharmodas-ghose-explained/), [The Lawscape](https://www.thelawscape.in/mohori-bibee-v-dharmodas-ghose-minor-contract-void/).
  - **Implication**: a minor cannot validly enter a paid Kissago subscription/coin-purchase contract in their own name — the contracting (and paying) party for kids-mode purchases needs to be an adult parent/guardian, as a matter of basic contract law, independent of any DPDP timing question below.
- **DPDP Act 2023, Section 9**: before processing a **child's** (under-18, Section 2(f)) personal data, a Data Fiduciary must obtain **verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian**; behavioural tracking/monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited outright, with limited exemptions (e.g., healthcare/education-specific processing) that likely don't cover a general entertainment app. SECONDARY (reliable): [DPDPA.com, Section 9 with interpretation](https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html), [The Quantum Hub](https://thequantumhub.com/navigating-childrens-privacy-and-parental-consent-under-the-dpdp-act-2023/).
- **DPDP Rules 2025 — commencement is phased, and the phasing matters a lot for how urgent this is**: notified by **G.S.R. 846(E), dated 13 November 2025** (published 14 November 2025). Three phases: **Phase 1 (13 Nov 2025)** — Data Protection Board constitution, definitions, complaint-filing machinery; **Phase 2 (13 Nov 2026)** — Consent Manager registration, enforcement/penalty machinery; **Phase 3 (13 May 2027)** — full operative compliance with notice, consent standards, Data Principal rights, security safeguards, breach reporting, **retention and deletion, and children's data** provisions. SECONDARY (reliable): multiple consistent sources including [PIB notification PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf), [Consently timeline](https://www.consently.in/blog/dpdp-rules-2025-implementation-timeline-india), [ProtectComply](https://protectcomply.com/blog/dpdp-rules-2025-timeline).
  - **ASK CA — an important nuance, not confidently resolved this session**: the *Rules'* operative mechanics for children's data (e.g., the verifiable-consent method prescribed under Rule 10) appear not to bind until **13 May 2027**. Whether the **Act's** Section 9 obligation is *already* independently enforceable now (since the Act was passed in 2023 and Section 1 commencement of individual provisions is separately notifiable) or effectively waits on the Rules to have teeth is a real legal question this research could not cleanly settle from secondary sources — **get an explicit CA opinion on the current enforceability date for Section 9**, rather than assuming either "already in force" or "safe until May 2027."
- **Product implication regardless of the exact enforceability date**: with **no age gate on checkout** today (per code audit) and a kids mode that presumably serves minors, Kissago currently has no mechanism to (a) ensure the paying/contracting party is an adult, or (b) obtain any parental consent for a linked minor's data processing. Given contract-law exposure alone is already live (independent of DPDP's phase-in), this is worth treating as a **near-term MUST**, not a 2027 problem: **SHOULD, trending MUST** — require the account that pays (and ideally the account that sets up a kids-mode/child profile) to affirmatively be an adult, with the specific parental-consent mechanism refined once the CA confirms the DPDP timing.
