# Product Purpose and Positioning

## The central problem

People have ideas, experiences, lessons and characters worth sharing, but turning them into a polished narrated visual story is still unnecessarily fragmented.

A person may need separate tools for:

- Writing
- Story structuring
- Image creation
- Voice recording or narration
- Timing
- Spoken text overlays
- Scene transitions
- Camera movement
- Special effects
- Video editing
- Exporting and sharing

This makes storytelling feel like a software-production task. Parents, educators, children and independent creators are often forced to spend more time learning tools than shaping the idea they wanted to communicate.

AI has accelerated content generation, but faster generation does not automatically lead to better storytelling. Content can become visually noisy, poorly paced, inconsistent or narratively weak. For children in particular, constant visual stimulation can replace calm attention, emotional progression and meaningful participation.

## Kissago's response

Kissago brings story development, images, narration, text overlays, transitions, movement and effects into one guided storytelling process.

The goal is not to remove human authorship. It is to reduce the technical burden around it.

Kissago allows a person to:

- Begin with a simple idea
- Shape it into story beats
- Build or adopt characters
- Generate visual scenes
- Add narration
- Synchronize readable text with the spoken narration
- Add restrained transitions and effects
- Refine individual parts
- Export or continue the story

A process that may otherwise require several applications can often be completed in approximately five to ten minutes, depending on the complexity of the story and the amount of refinement.

## The responsibility layer

Kissago should not present stimulation as a substitute for engagement.

The product should communicate:

- Calm can still be visually rich
- Pace should support the story
- Effects should strengthen meaning
- Parents and educators should have control
- Character consistency matters
- Narrative progression matters
- The user should be able to review and refine generated material
- AI should support intention rather than replace it

## The creative expansion

Kissago begins with accessible story creation, but it can grow with the creator.

A user can move from:

- One story
- To a recurring character
- To a sequence of episodes
- To a connected story world
- To a recognisable creative identity or intellectual property

This makes Kissago relevant to parents and educators while also opening meaningful space for creators, institutions and collaborators.

## Presentation tone

The `/learn` route should sound:

- Clear rather than promotional
- Thoughtful rather than alarmist
- Warm rather than childish
- Imaginative rather than noisy
- Responsible rather than restrictive
- Ambitious without claiming unavailable functionality
- Accessible without oversimplifying the platform

## Product promise

Suggested primary promise:

**Turn an idea into a narrated visual story.**

Suggested supporting promise:

**Kissago brings writing, imagery, narration, spoken text, movement and effects into one guided creative journey.**

Suggested responsibility statement:

**Create stories that hold attention without competing for it.**
